## Referee report and independent derivation
For a total dominating set of size k with c components, each component has at least two vertices. Partition the rest among neighboring components. A quotient spanning tree connects them by at most 2(c-1) new vertices; attaching the remaining vertices as leaves gives diameter <=k+2c-1<=2k-1. If k odd, <=2k-2. Thus radius<=k; when equality holds the spanning tree is bicentral with an adjacent pair of graph centers, forcing the mean strictly below k. The proof needs the connected hypothesis explicitly in the lemma; the singleton case belongs to the radius<k branch.
For k=2, a dominating edge has endpoints of eccentricity <=2. If radius=1, the center is a clique; else that edge dominates the induced center. Hence center pair distances are 1 or 2 and sum=2M-e, e>=m-1. The stated K_{2,m-1} plus leaf construction realizes every edge count by adding edges among the m-1 vertices; the two noncentral vertices remain at distance 3. A diametral pair lies outside a proper center. The marked four-vertex construction and obstruction were checked by the stated adjacency/distance arguments, including distinctness of the four witnesses. For the no-C4 case, exclusive sides of a dominating edge have distance 3; only the endpoints and at most one shared neighbor can be central. No general even-k>=4 result claimed.

## Independent experiment

Observed 995 nonisomorphic connected graphs, 0 failures of #268; 179 odd-k graphs, 0 failures of the odd bound; 794 k=2 graphs (639 with >=2 centers, 0 failures of spectrum bound or induced-center dominating-edge necessity); 38 k=2 graphs without any C4, 0 failures of mean<=1.
